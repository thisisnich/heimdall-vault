Digital Modulation Scheme – BPSK
BPSK Modulator
There are two switches, S1 and S2 in the diagram. Whether the switches are on or off are depend on the  data bits (100 Hz). For example, when the data is 1, switch S1 will be on and switch S2 will be off.
The carrier wave being higher frequency (1KHz) has two signals. One signal is in phase and another one is 180 degree out of phase. Which carrier signal (in phase or out of phase) appears on the output depends on which switch is on,
When the data is one, switch 1 will be on and switch 2 will be off. When switch 1 is on, the carrier signal, in phase will appear at the output.
When the data is zero, switch 2 will be on and switch 1 will be off. When switch 2 is on, the carrier signal, out of phase will appear at the output

Digital Modulation Scheme – M-ary QPSK
QPSK Modulator
In the QPSK modulator, the incoming bit-stream is divided into two parallel streams to be fed simultaneously to the I and Q balanced modulators to construct the 2 bit symbols. The carrier output from the modulator is controlled by the digital bit stream and by adding together the I and Q outputs, the phase state diagram is generated.

The LPF is a baseband filter (Raised Cosine filter) used to shape the spectrum of the incoming pulses. The filter at the output of the modulator confines the power spectrum of the QPSK signal within the allocated band. This prevents spill-over of signal energy into adjacent channels and also removes out-of-band spurious signals generated during the modulation process.

Digital Modulation Scheme
Raise Cosine
Raised-cosine filter used for pulse-shaping in digital modulation.
The purpose is to minimise intersymbol interference (ISI).

ISI interferes with subsequent symbols and has similar effect as noise.

The spreading of the pulse is beyond its allotted time interval and hence it causes to interfere with neighbouring pulses.

Digital Modulation Applications
Factors Affecting Choice of Modulation
1) Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)

2) Probability of error or Bit Error Rate (BER)

3) Power Efficiency

Power efficiency is a measure of how much received power is needed to achieve a specified BER (inversely proportional to BER)

As BER increases, power efficiency decreases since transmitted power is “wasted” on more bad data

4) Bandwidth Efficiency (or Spectral Efficiency)
Defined as the ratio of the bit rate to the channel bandwidth
The capacity of a digital system is directly related to bandwidth efficiency
